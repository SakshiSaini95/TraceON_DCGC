## 1. What is Traceon?
Traceon is a tool that helps you check a seller before the money moves. Its tagline is "Check the seller. Before the money moves." Traceon links the clues hiding in plain sight, so you can trust the person behind the profile. It is aimed at people buying from sellers on social platforms such as Instagram and WhatsApp.

Q: What does Traceon do?
A: It connects public signals about a seller (profiles, phone numbers, UPI IDs, photos, links) and gives you a risk verdict, low, medium or high, with plain-language reasons and evidence, before you pay.

Q: Who is Tracy?
A: Tracy is Traceon's mascot and assistant, a small dark character with glowing lime eyes who helps people trace the signal.

Q: What does "trace the signal" mean?
A: It's Traceon's way of saying: follow the clues connected to a seller to see whether they add up.

## 2. How it works (3 steps)
1. **Paste or peek.** Open a profile, or paste a link, phone number, UPI ID or photo.
2. **We link the clues.** OCR, image fingerprints and entity linking connect the dots between profiles, numbers and photos.
3. **Get the verdict.** Low, medium or high risk, with reasons you can actually understand.

Q: What can I check?
A: A profile link, a phone number, a UPI ID or a photo.

Q: What is OCR / image fingerprinting / entity linking?
A: OCR reads text inside images (for example numbers or names in screenshots). Image fingerprints let Traceon recognise when the same photo shows up on other shops. Entity linking connects the same person's details (usernames, phone numbers, photos) across different profiles.

Q: How fast is it?
A: Traceon is built to return results in seconds.

Q: Do I need an account?
A: No account is needed to try the demo on the site. ("No account needed" is one of Traceon's promises.)

Q: Do I have to wait for someone to complain first?
A: No. Traceon doesn't wait for a complaint; it checks signals before you pay.

## 5. Risk levels
Traceon gives a risk level with a score out of 100 and reasons:
- **Low risk:** no linked red flags found.
- **Medium risk:** nothing is linked to known scams, but something doesn't add up, for example a new account with little history to check.
- **High risk:** several warning signs line up, for example the same phone number or photos appearing across multiple shops.

Q: Does "high risk" mean the seller is definitely a scammer?
A: No. It means several warning signs were found and you should look closer before paying. It is a signal, not proof of guilt, and no tool can guarantee a seller is safe or unsafe.

Q: Does "low risk" mean it's safe to pay?
A: Not a guarantee. It means no linked red flags were found. Still use protected payment methods where possible and use your judgement.

Q: Examples of signals Traceon may show:
A: Phone number shared by several profiles, photos reused from other shops, a very new account, a UPI name that doesn't match the shop name, no verifiable reviews, or no complaints on record.

## 12. Safe buying tips (general advice Tracy may give)
- Slow down. Pressure to pay "right now" is a classic warning sign.
- Check the seller before paying: run the profile, number or UPI ID through Traceon.
- Look for reused photos: reverse-search product pictures.
- Be cautious if the UPI/bank account name doesn't match the shop or person name.
- Prefer payment methods with buyer protection; be wary of seller requests to pay by gift card, crypto or "friends & family" transfers.
- Be careful with very new accounts, no history, deleted comments, or disabled reviews.
- Ask for a live video of the product or a small test order for larger purchases.
- Never share OTPs, PINs, passwords or card details with anyone.
- Keep screenshots, chats and receipts as evidence.

## 13. If you think you've been scammed
Tracy should be empathetic, never judgemental:
1. Stop sending any more money.
2. Save screenshots, chat history, profile links and payment receipts.
3. Contact your bank or UPI/payment app immediately and report the transaction.
4. Report the profile on the platform (Instagram/WhatsApp).
5. Report to local cybercrime authorities. In India: the national cybercrime reporting portal or helpline 1930.
Traceon cannot recover money or act as law enforcement.
